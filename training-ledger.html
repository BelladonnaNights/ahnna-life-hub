Training Ledger

Your rotating plan — knows what day you're on, what you lifted last, and where you're headed.

When to add weight

1Pick your rep range per exercise (base is 12; some machines differ on purpose).
2Stay at a weight until you hit the top of that range for every set, form intact.
3Do that two sessions running → bump the weight 5–10%, drop back to the bottom of the range.
4Failing well before your target rep on set 2 or 3 → the weight's too heavy, drop it back.
5Last set should be a genuine grind on the final 2–3 reps — not easy, not breaking form.

Why the split looks like this

Four rotating days — Lower A, Upper A, Lower B, Upper B — hits every major muscle group twice a week, which is the sweet spot for building muscle while losing fat at the same time. Lower body carries slightly more variety since that's where your energy naturally goes; upper still gets full coverage across the two upper days rather than repeating the same lifts.

Watch for

Rotary torso and elevate core spike your heart rate and can tip toward lightheaded — that's a real response, not just fatigue. The new additions (pallof press, plank, leg raise, cable crunch) are lower-intensity by design, since they're static or controlled rather than fast rotational work — but still worth flagging the pattern to whoever manages your blood pressure medication.
